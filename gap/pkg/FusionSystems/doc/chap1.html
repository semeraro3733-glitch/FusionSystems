1 Introduction

1.1 Fusion systems

Let S be a finite p-group. A fusion system on S is a category F whose objects are the subgroups of S and whose morphisms Hom_F(P,Q) are injective group homomorphisms containing all those induced by conjugation in S, such that every morphism factors as an isomorphism followed by an inclusion. The fusion system generated by subgroups A_i ≤ Aut(E_i) (E_i ≤ S) is the smallest fusion system containing them. By the Alperin–Goldschmidt theorem a saturated fusion system is generated by Aut_F(S) together with the groups Aut_F(E), E running through representatives of the F-conjugacy classes of essential subgroups (the fully normalized F-centric subgroups E for which Out_F(E) has a strongly p-embedded subgroup).

In this package a fusion system is therefore given by the data

  • S, a p-group given as a pc group,

  • automorphisms of S which together with Inn(S) generate Aut_F(S),

  • a list of subgroups E_1,dots,E_k of S (the essential subgroups) and, for each of them, a list of automorphisms generating Aut_F(E_i).

The fusion system is the one generated by these groups; it is not assumed to be saturated. Whether it is saturated is decided by IsSaturated (2.3-1).

1.2 Overview of the algorithms

Essential subgroups. By [Gau26, Theorem 3.6] every essential subgroup of a saturated fusion system on S is S-conjugate to one of the subgroups C_S(xA/A), where A runs through the terms of a central series of S with factors of order p refining the lower central series and contained in [S,S], and xA through the elements of order p of S/A. These candidates are filtered with the tests of [PS21]; all tests are invariant under Aut(S) and the expensive ones (which need Aut(P)) are applied once per Aut(S)-orbit. The subgroup lattice of S is never computed.

Automorphism groups. Automorphism groups of p-groups are computed with the package autpgrp. If Aut(P) is soluble all computations with its subgroups take place in the pc group returned by PcGroupAutPGroup; otherwise a permutation representation is used.

Fusion. Let B = Aut_F(S) ⋉ S and let C be a p'-complement of Inn(S) in Aut_F(S). Every isomorphism in F is a composite of restrictions of elements of Aut_F(S) and of Aut_F(E_i^s), s ∈ S. The S-conjugacy classes of subgroups form the nodes of a graph which is built lazily: at a node with representative R and for every S-conjugate E^s of an essential subgroup containing R we record the Schreier graph of the orbit of R under Aut_F(E^s), with the arrows conjugated into the class representatives, and the arrows R -> c(R) for the generators c of C. By the Reidemeister–Schreier procedure for groupoids the F-class of R is the connected component of its node and Aut_F(R) is generated by the conjugates of the groups Aut_S(X), X a node of the component, and the loops of the component.

Saturation. By [PS21, Theorem 2.3] (which rests on [BCG+05, Theorem 2.2]) a fusion system generated by Aut_F(S) and the automizers of F-centric subgroups is saturated if and only if Inn(S) is a Sylow p-subgroup of Aut_F(S), every fully normalized F-centric subgroup P is fully automized, and every fully normalized F-centric F-radical P has the surjectivity property: for all P < V ≤ N_S(P) the restriction map N_Aut_F(V)(P) -> N_Aut_F(P)(Aut_V(P)) is surjective. Let T be the set of subgroups of S contained in a B-conjugate of an essential subgroup. T is closed under F-conjugacy and under taking subgroups, and for P ∉ T every F-morphism with source P is the restriction of a conjugation by an element of B (the automizers of the essential subgroups never apply). Hence the F-class of P, Aut_F(P) and the extension condition at P are those of the saturated fusion system F_S(B), and the conditions hold. It therefore suffices to run the test over the F-classes of the S-centric subgroups of the essential subgroups. These are found from the subgroup lattices of the groups E_i/Z(E_i), their F-classes and automizers from the lazy graph. A class is only expanded as far as it is needed: the test abandons the F-class of an essential subgroup, or of an S-centric subgroup of one, at the first member which is not S-centric or has a larger normalizer in S, as such a member already decides the condition in question. (For a candidate system which is far from saturated such a class may be enormous, for instance when a subgroup of an abelian essential subgroup with a large automizer lies in it, while its first bad member is found at once.) This is how the package avoids the fusion graph of [PS21], whose construction (the subgroup lattice of S up to B-conjugacy and the Aut_F(E)-orbits of all subgroups of all essential subgroups) is the most expensive part of the original implementation.

Classification. AllFusionSystems (3.2-1) follows [PS21, Section 4]: for each p'-subgroup K of Aut(S) up to conjugacy (Aut_F(S) = Inn(S) ⋊ K) the proto-essential subgroups are grouped into B-classes; the candidates for Aut_F(P) are the subgroups A of Aut(P) with Aut_S(P) as Sylow p-subgroup and A/Inn(P) having a strongly p-embedded subgroup, one for each Aut(P)-class (all conjugates of such a subgroup containing Aut_S(P) as a Sylow p-subgroup are conjugate under N_Aut(P)(Aut_S(P))), expanded to the conjugates whose Sylow normalizer contains Aut_N_B(P)(P), with equality when N_S(P) lies in no B-conjugate of a proto-essential subgroup; the subsets of proto-essentials and the assignments of automizers are run through up to the action of N_Aut(S)(Aut_F(S)); the resulting systems are tested for O^p(F)=F, O_p(F)=1 and saturation and finally reduced up to isomorphism. The automizer candidates are enumerated by the cyclic extension method with the hereditary bound |H|_p ≤ |Out_S(P)|; this is complete whenever GAP can handle the subgroup lattice of Out(P). For elementary abelian P with a huge Out(P) = GL(n,p) the package falls back to the monomial groups of the Aut_S(P)-invariant frames (and reports this through InfoFusionSystems (3.2-2)), or uses candidates supplied by the user through the option candidates.
