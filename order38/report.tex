\large a running report}

\begin{abstract}
We search for the saturated fusion systems $\F$ on the groups $S$ of order
$3^8$ with $O_3(\F)=1$ and $O^3(\F)=\F$, in the manner of
Parker--Semeraro \cite{PS} but with the fast proto-essential search of
Gautam \cite{Gautam} and the fusion-graph-free saturation test of the
GAP package \texttt{FusionSystems} that accompanies this report.  There are
$1\,396\,077$ groups of order $3^8$ (Vaughan-Lee's database, available in
GAP through the package \texttt{sglppow}).  Section~\ref{sec:reduction}
reduces them to a short list by necessary conditions, Section~\ref{sec:groups}
identifies the surviving groups, Section~\ref{sec:lookup} contains the
look-up tables of subgroups of $\GL_n(3)$ with a strongly $3$-embedded
subgroup, and Section~\ref{sec:results} records the fusion systems found
group by group.  This document is updated as the computation proceeds; the
current state is summarised in Section~\ref{sec:status}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conventions and the strategy}\label{sec:strategy}

Throughout $p=3$, $S$ is a group of order $3^8$ and $\F$ is a saturated
fusion system on $S$ with $O_3(\F)=1$ and $O^3(\F)=\F$.  We write
$\Aut_S(E)=N_S(E)/C_S(E)$ and $\Out_S(E)=\Aut_S(E)\Inn(E)/\Inn(E)$; for an
$S$-centric subgroup $E$ (that is $C_S(E)\le E$) we have
$\Out_S(E)\cong N_S(E)/E$.

By the Alperin--Goldschmidt fusion theorem $\F$ is generated by $\Aut_\F(S)$
and the groups $\Aut_\F(E)$ for the $\F$-essential subgroups $E$ (the
$\F$-centric, fully normalised subgroups with $\Out_\F(E)$ having a strongly
$3$-embedded subgroup).  Following \cite{PS} we therefore proceed as
follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Determine the subgroups $E\le S$ which can be essential in some
  saturated fusion system on $S$ (the \emph{proto-essential} subgroups), up
  to $\Aut(S)$-conjugacy.  Here we use Gautam's theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:gautam}
  below) instead of the subgroup lattice of $S$.
\item Determine the Borel subgroups, i.e.\ the $3'$-subgroups $K\le\Aut(S)$
  up to conjugacy, so that $\Aut_\F(S)=\Inn(S)K$ (recall that $\Inn(S)$ is a
  normal Sylow $3$-subgroup of $\Aut_\F(S)$).
\item For each Borel subgroup and each proto-essential subgroup $E$ determine
  the candidates for $\Aut_\F(E)$: the subgroups of $\Aut(E)$ containing
  $\Inn(E)$ with $\Aut_S(E)$ as a Sylow $3$-subgroup, with $\Out$ having a
  strongly $3$-embedded subgroup and compatible with the Borel subgroup.
\item For each choice of a set of essential subgroups (up to the action of
  $N_{\Aut(S)}(\Aut_\F(S))$) and automizers, build the fusion system,
  discard it unless $O^3(\F)=\F$, $O_3(\F)=1$ and $\F$ is saturated, and
  finally remove isomorphic copies.
\end{enumerate}
Steps 2--4 are only carried out for the groups surviving the reduction of
Section~\ref{sec:reduction}.  All computations are done with the GAP package
\texttt{FusionSystems}; the scripts for this report are in the directory
\texttt{order38/scripts} of the repository.

\begin{theorem}[Gautam {\cite[Theorem 3.6]{Gautam}}]\label{thm:gautam}
Let $S=L_1>L_2>\dots>L_m=1$ be a central series of $S$ with all factors
of order $p$ which refines the lower central series.  Every essential
subgroup of a saturated fusion system on $S$ is $S$-conjugate to a subgroup
$C_S(xA/A)=\{s\in S : [s,x]\in A\}$ with $A=L_i\le [S,S]$ and $xA$ an
element of order $p$ of $S/A$.
\end{theorem}

\section{The reduction}\label{sec:reduction}

\subsection{Necessary conditions}

We use the following necessary conditions on $S$.  Conditions
(R1)--(R3) involve only $S$, its candidate subgroups $E$ and (for the few
candidates passing the structural tests) $\Aut(E)$; they are applied to all
$1\,396\,077$ groups (Stage 1a).  Conditions (R4)--(R6) need $\Aut(S)$ and
are applied to the survivors (Stage 1b).

\begin{lemma}[{\cite[Lemma 7.1]{PS}}]\label{lem:R1}
{\rm (R1)} If $|S:\Z(S)|\le 9$ then $S$ carries no saturated fusion system
with $O_3(\F)=1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:R2}
{\rm (R2)} Let $E$ be an essential subgroup of a saturated fusion system
$\F$ on $S$ and $T=\Out_S(E)\cong N_S(E)/E$.  Then
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $E$ is $S$-centric and $E<N_S(E)$;
\item[(b)] $T$ is a Sylow $3$-subgroup of a group with a strongly
  $3$-embedded subgroup, so $T$ is cyclic, or elementary abelian of rank at
  least $2$, or $T\cong 3^{1+2}_+$, or $T\cong\Syl_3(\PSU_3(9))$ of order
  $3^6$;
\item[(c)] $|E/\Phifr(E)|\ge |T|^2$;
\item[(d)] if $1=D_0<D_1<\dots<D_k=E$ is a chain of characteristic
  subgroups of $E$, then no element of $N_S(E)\setminus E$ centralises every
  factor $D_{i}/D_{i-1}$;
\item[(e)] $\Aut(E)$ is not a $3$-group, $\Aut_S(E)\cap O_3(\Aut(E))=\Inn(E)$,
  and $\Aut(E)$ is insoluble unless $T$ is cyclic;
\item[(f)] if $T$ is not cyclic then $|\Aut(E)|$ is divisible by $5$ for
  $T\cong 3^2$, by $7\cdot13$ for $T\cong3^3$, by $7$ for $T\cong3^{1+2}_+$,
  by $5\cdot41$ for $T\cong3^4$, by $11\cdot61$ for $T\cong3^5$ and by
  $5\cdot73$ for $|T|=3^6$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(a) and (b) are part of the definition and the classification of the
groups with a strongly $p$-embedded subgroup (for $p=3$ and $T$ non-cyclic
the possibilities for $O^{3'}(\Out_\F(E)/O_{3'}(\Out_\F(E)))$ are
$\PSL_2(3^n)$, $\PSU_3(3^n)$, ${}^2G_2(3^n)$, $\PSL_3(4)$ and $M_{11}$; the
Ree groups have Sylow $3$-subgroups of order at least $3^9$).  (c) is
\cite[Lemma 2.?]{PS}: $\Out_\F(E)$ acts faithfully on $E/\Phifr(E)$ because
the kernel of $\Aut(E)\to\Aut(E/\Phifr(E))$ is a normal $3$-subgroup and
$O_3(\Out_\F(E))=1$ as $E$ is $\F$-radical.  (d) The automorphisms of $E$
centralising all factors of a chain of characteristic subgroups form a
normal subgroup $K$ of $\Aut(E)$, and $K$ is a $3$-group (a $3'$-automorphism
centralising all factors of a normal series of a $3$-group is trivial).
Hence $K\Inn(E)/\Inn(E)\le O_3(\Out(E))$ meets $\Out_\F(E)$ in a normal
$3$-subgroup, which is trivial, so $K\cap\Aut_S(E)\le\Inn(E)$, which is
(d) as $E$ is $S$-centric.  (e) is the $\F$-radical condition
$O_3(\Out_\F(E))=1$ together with the fact that $T\ne1$ is not normal in
$\Out_\F(E)$ (a group with a strongly $3$-embedded subgroup has more than
one Sylow $3$-subgroup), so that $\Out(E)$ is not a $3$-group; if $T$ is not
cyclic then $O^{3'}(\Out_\F(E)/O_{3'}(\Out_\F(E)))$ is a non-abelian simple
group by the classification quoted in (b), so $\Aut(E)$ is insoluble.
(f) That simple group is a section of $\Out(E)$ and is one of $\PSL_2(9)$,
$\PSL_3(4)$, $M_{11}$ for $T\cong3^2$, $\PSL_2(27)$ for $3^3$,
$\PSU_3(3)$ for $3^{1+2}_+$, $\PSL_2(81)$ for $3^4$, $\PSL_2(243)$ for
$3^5$ and $\PSL_2(729)$ or $\PSU_3(9)$ for $|T|=3^6$; the primes listed
divide the orders of all groups in the respective list.
\end{proof}

In Stage 1a a subgroup $E$ is called a \emph{candidate} if it is
$S$-conjugate to a subgroup $C_S(xA/A)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:gautam} and
satisfies (a)--(f) of Lemma~\ref{lem:R2}, where in (d) we use the chain
$E>\Phifr(E)>1$ and then the covering pairs of the set of characteristic
subgroups generated (by one round of intersections and products) by
$\Z(E)$, $\Z_2(E)$, $\Phifr(E)$, $E'$, $\gamma_3(E)$, $\mho_1(E)$,
$\Omega_1(\Z(E))$, $\Z(\Phifr(E))$ and $C_E(E')$.  (The stability group of
the set of covering pairs is contained in the stability group of any
maximal chain in the set, so the argument of (d) applies.)  The tests are
applied in this order, so that $\Aut(E)$ (computed with the GAP package
\texttt{autpgrp}) is only needed for the candidates surviving (a)--(d);
the first part of (e) discards a good part of them, since many groups of
order $3^7$ have a $3$-group of automorphisms.  With
\texttt{autpgrp} we computed the automorphism groups of all groups of order
$3^k$, $k\le7$ (\texttt{data/aut3table.g}):

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:R3}
{\rm (R3)} If $W\ne 1$ is a characteristic subgroup of $S$ which is contained
in and characteristic in every candidate, then $W\le O_3(\F)$ for every
saturated fusion system $\F$ on $S$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$W$ is normalised by $\Aut_\F(S)$ and by $\Aut_\F(E)$ for every essential
$E$ (an essential subgroup is $S$-conjugate to a candidate $E_0$, and $W=W^s$
is characteristic in $E_0^s$).  By Alperin--Goldschmidt $W$ is normalised
by every morphism of $\F$ defined on it, i.e.\ $W\trianglelefteq\F$.
\end{proof}
In Stage 1a ``characteristic'' is replaced by the sufficient condition of
membership in the sets of visibly characteristic subgroups described above.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:R456}
Let $\mathcal{E}$ be a set of representatives of the $S$-classes of the
proto-essential subgroups of $S$: the candidates of Stage 1a, where a
candidate whose $\Aut(S)$-orbit contains a subgroup which is not a
candidate is discarded (all the conditions of Lemma~\ref{lem:R2} are
$\Aut(S)$-invariant).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(R4)] If $\mathcal{E}=\emptyset$ then $O_3(\F)=S$ for every saturated
  $\F$ on $S$.
\item[(R5)] If a nontrivial characteristic subgroup of $S$ is characteristic
  in every member of $\mathcal{E}$, then $O_3(\F)\neq1$.
\item[(R6)] If $S\ne\gen{[S,O^{3}(\Aut(S))],\,E : E\in\mathcal{E}}$ then
  $O^3(\F)<\F$ for every saturated $\F$ on $S$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(R4) and (R5) are as before.  (R6): $O^3(\F)=\F$ if and only if the
hyperfocal subgroup $\gen{[P,O^3(\Aut_\F(P))] : P\le S}$ is $S$, and by
Alperin--Goldschmidt it is generated by $[S,O^3(\Aut_\F(S))]$ and the
$[E,O^3(\Aut_\F(E))]\le E$ for $E$ essential; finally $O^3(\Aut_\F(S))\le
O^3(\Aut(S))$.
\end{proof}

\section{Look-up tables: subgroups of $\GL_n(3)$ with a strongly $3$-embedded subgroup}\label{sec:lookup}

If $E\cong 3^n$ is an elementary abelian essential subgroup then
$\Out_\F(E)=\Aut_\F(E)\le\GL_n(3)$ has Sylow $3$-subgroup $T=\Aut_S(E)$
of order at most $3^{8-n}$ (as $E$ is centric and $N_S(E)>E$, in fact
$n\le 7$) and a strongly $3$-embedded subgroup.  The following tables list,
for each $\GL_n(3)$-class of admissible $T$ (cyclic, elementary abelian or
$3^{1+2}_+$), the subgroups $H$ with $T\in\Syl_3(H)$ and a strongly
$3$-embedded subgroup, up to conjugacy in $N_{\GL_n(3)}(T)$.  For $n\le4$ the
tables are complete.  They are produced by \texttt{scripts/gltables.g} and
stored in machine-readable form in \texttt{data/gl$n$\_table.g}.

The classes of $T$ are distinguished in the tables by $|N_{\GL_n(3)}(T)|$;
when the same $H$ appears several times for one $T$ the copies are not
conjugate under $N_{\GL_n(3)}(T)$ (they differ in the way $H$ acts, e.g.\ in
the determinant or in the composition factors).  The column ``factors''
gives the dimensions of the composition factors of $H$ on the natural
module, so that e.g.\ $1+2$ for $\SL_2(3)\le\GL_3(3)$ means the module has a
trivial submodule with irreducible $2$-dimensional quotient.  For $n\le 4$
the only candidates with a non-cyclic Sylow $3$-subgroup occur for two
classes of $T\cong 3^2$ in $\GL_4(3)$: the extensions of $\Alt(6)\cong

\paragraph{Completeness of the automizer candidates.}  For an essential
subgroup $E$ the candidates for $\Out_\F(E)$ are complete in the following
cases: $|\Out(E)|\le 5\cdot10^5$ (or $\Out(E)$ soluble of order at most
$10^9$), where the subgroup lattice of $\Out(E)$ is used; $E$ elementary
abelian of rank at most $4$ (the tables above); $E$ elementary abelian of
rank $5$ with $\Out_S(E)\cong3^2$ (the table above); and every $E$ with
$\Out_S(E)$ neither cyclic nor quaternion (then $\Out_\F(E)$ is not
$3$-soluble and the perfect subgroup method of the package is complete).
For cyclic $\Out_S(E)=T$ and $\Out(E)$ beyond the lattice bound the
$3$-soluble candidates are now enumerated completely as well
(\texttt{FS\_PSolubleCyclicCandidates}): such a candidate $H$ has
$Q=O_{3'}(H)\ne1$ with $T$ acting faithfully on $Q$, and $H/Q$ has a
normal Sylow $3$-subgroup, so $H=QT'R$ with $T'$ a conjugate of $T$
normalising $Q$ and $R$ a $3'$-subgroup of $N(QT')$.  The $3'$-subgroups
$Q$ of $G=\Out(E)$ are found through the soluble radical (the $3'$-subgroups
of $G/\mathrm{Rad}(G)$ from its subgroup lattice, then the Hall
$3'$-subgroups of their soluble preimages, which contain every $3'$-subgroup),
the conjugates $T'$ from the conjugacy classes of $N_G(Q)$ when this group
is small and as the fixed points of $T$ on the cosets of $N_G(Q)$
otherwise.  The method applies whenever $G/\mathrm{Rad}(G)$ has a feasible
subgroup lattice, which is always the case for the non-abelian
proto-essential subgroups met so far (where $G/\mathrm{Rad}(G)$ is a
subgroup of $\GL_d(3)$, $d\le5$, such as $\PSL_3(3)$); a soluble $\Out(E)$
with non-cyclic $\Out_S(E)$ has no candidates at all.  The only remaining
incomplete case is an elementary abelian $E$ of rank $5$, $6$ or $7$ with
$\Out_S(E)$ cyclic and $\Out(E)=\GL_n(3)$ (where $G/\mathrm{Rad}(G)$ is
$\PGL_n(3)$ itself), where the $3$-soluble candidates are only found
inside the monomial groups of the $T$-invariant frames; the package
records every incomplete enumeration in \texttt{FS\_Diagnostics}, and the
Stage 2 logs and tables mark the affected groups.  Results for groups whose only proto-essential
subgroups of this kind are elementary abelian of rank at least $5$ with
cyclic $\Out_S(E)$ are therefore marked ``complete up to the soluble
candidates'' below; such groups are, by the ordering of Stage 2, treated
last.  (For $\Out_S(E)\cong C_9$ the subgroup $\Omega_1(T)$ must act nontrivially
on $O_{3'}(H)$, so $T$ acts faithfully on a $\{2,5,11,13\}$-group inside
$\GL_n(3)$; a faithful action of $C_9$ needs a $2$-section of rank at least
$6$, a $5$- or $11$-section of rank at least $6$ or a $13$-section of rank at
least $3$, none of which exists in $\GL_5(3)$.  So for $n=5$ and cyclic
$\Out_S(E)$ of order $9$ there are no $3$-soluble candidates, and the
incompleteness only concerns $\Out_S(E)\cong C_3$ for $n=5$ and cyclic
$\Out_S(E)$ for $n=6,7$.)

\paragraph{Use of the tables.}  In Stage 1a an elementary abelian candidate
$E\cong3^n$ is discarded unless $\Aut_S(E)$ is conjugate in $\GL_n(3)$ to a
class with candidates ($n\le4$, and $n=5$ for $\Out_S(E)\cong3^2$), and in
Stage 2 the automizer candidates for such $E$ are taken from the tables
(transported along the conjugating matrix) instead of being enumerated
anew for each group.  This matters for correctness as well as for speed: for
$|\GL_n(3)|$ beyond the lattice bound the package's own enumeration of the
soluble candidates for cyclic $\Out_S(E)$ (the frame method) is not
complete, e.g.\ it misses the irreducible $13{:}3\le\GL_3(3)\le\GL_4(3)$.


\section{Results group by group}\label{sec:results}

Each subsection treats one group $S$ and lists the saturated fusion
systems $\F$ on $S$ with $O_3(\F)=1$ and $O^3(\F)=\F$ found by
\texttt{AllFusionSystems}, up to isomorphism.  For each $\F$ we give
$\Out_\F(S)$, the essential subgroups $E$ up to $\F$-conjugacy (with
$|N_S(E):E|$) and $\Out_\F(E)$, in the style of the tables of \cite{PS}.
Identifications with fusion systems of finite groups are proved by
\texttt{IsIsomorphicFusionSystems} against the fusion system of the group
when the group is available in GAP, and are otherwise indicated as
expectations.  The groups are treated in the order of difficulty (the
order of the largest elementary abelian proto-essential subgroup).

\subsection{The Sylow 3-subgroup of $\PSL_3(q)$, $3^4\,\|\,q-1$, and of $\PSU_3(q)$, $3^4\,\|\,q+1$: \texttt{SmallGroup(6561,2221)}}
\label{sec:psl3}

Let $T_3=\{\mathrm{diag}(a,b,c): a,b,c\in C_{81},\ abc=1\}\cong C_{81}^2$, let
$c$ be the permutation matrix of a $3$-cycle and $Z=\langle\omega I\rangle$
($\omega$ a primitive cube root of unity).  Then
$S=(T_3\rtimes\langle c\rangle)/Z$ is \texttt{SmallGroup(6561,2221)}
(verified with \texttt{IsomorphismGroups}); it is the Sylow $3$-subgroup of
$\PSL_3(q)$ for $3^4\,\|\,q-1$ (the smallest case is $q=163$) and of
$\PSU_3(q)$ for $3^4\,\|\,q+1$ (smallest case $q=647$).  It has maximal
class, $|\Z(S)|=3$, exponent $81$, the abelian maximal subgroup
$A=T_3/Z\cong C_{27}\times C_{81}$, all $4374$ elements of $S\setminus A$
have order $3$, and $|\Aut(S)|=2^2\cdot3^{13}$.  Let $z$ generate $\Z(S)$
(the image of $d=\mathrm{diag}(1,\omega,\omega^2)$).

\emph{Proto-essential subgroups.}  An $S$-centric elementary abelian
subgroup of order $9$ contains $z$, hence is $V=\langle z,x\rangle$ with
$x\in S\setminus A$ of order $3$.  There are exactly three $S$-classes
$V_1,V_2,V_3$ of such subgroups (one for each $A$-class of elements of the
coset $cA$; note $z\in[A,S]$), each with $|N_S(V_i):V_i|=3$, and
$\Aut(S)$ permutes them transitively.  By the $\GL_2(3)$ table,
$\Out_\F(V_i)\in\{\SL_2(3),\GL_2(3)\}$.  The remaining candidates are three
$S$-classes (one $\Aut(S)$-class) of subgroups $E\cong3^{1+2}_+$ with
$|N_S(E):E|=3$.  There are five Borel subgroups, $|\Out_\F(S)|\in\{1,2,2,2,4\}$;
the Borel with $\Out_\F(S)\cong C_2^2$ fixes one of the three classes of
rank two subgroups, which we call $V_1$, and interchanges $V_2$ and $V_3$
(so $V_2$ and $V_3$ are $\F$-conjugate whenever $\Out_\F(S)\cong C_2^2$).
The computation (\texttt{AllFusionSystems}, $17$ seconds) gives exactly
$7$ fusion systems.  In the table the essential subgroups are listed up to
$\F$-conjugacy; $[2]$ marks an $\F$-class consisting of two $S$-classes.

\begin{tabular}{cllll}
\toprule
$\F$ & $\Out_\F(S)$ & essential subgroups $E$ (all with $|N_S(E):E|=3$) & $\Out_\F(E)$ & realised by\\
\midrule
1 & $C_2$ & $V_1$, $V_2$, $V_3$ & $\SL_2(3)$ each & $\PSL_3(163)$, $\PSU_3(647)$\\
2 & $C_2$ & two of the $V_i$ & $\SL_2(3)$ each & \textcolor{red}{none known (exotic?)}\\

\emph{Identification.}  For $G=\PSL_3(q)$ every element of order $3$ in
$S\setminus A$ has eigenvalues $1,\omega,\omega^2$ and is therefore
$G$-conjugate to $z$; the preimage of each $V_i$ in $\SL_3(q)$ is the
Heisenberg group $3^{1+2}_+$ and $N_G(V_i)\cong 3^2{:}\SL_2(3)$ (the Hessian
group, which lies in $\PSL_3(q)$ exactly when $9\mid q-1$), so all three
$V_i$ are essential in $\F_S(G)$ with $\Out_G(V_i)\cong\SL_2(3)$,
$\Out_G(S)\cong C_2$ (the transposition of the Weyl group), and no
$3^{1+2}_+$ is essential.  The graph automorphism normalises one of the three
classes, where it enlarges the automiser to $\GL_2(3)$, and interchanges
the other two.  The unitary case is identical with $q+1$ in place of $q-1$.
These descriptions were confirmed by computing $\F_S(G)$ for
$G=\PSL_3(163)$, $\PSL_3(163){:}2$ (graph automorphism), $\PSU_3(647)$ and
$\PSU_3(647){:}2$ (field automorphism) and testing isomorphism with the
seven systems (\texttt{scripts/reference\_l3u3.g}, log
\texttt{logs/reference\_l3u3.log}; $3$ to $40$ seconds per group).  Since
$\PSL_3(163)$ has smallest permutation degree $26\,733$ and $\PSU_3(647)$ is
far beyond any permutation representation, the groups are never
constructed: for $U\le S$ with $z\in\Z(U)$ the normaliser $N_G(U)$ is
generated by $N_{C_G(z)}(U)$, where $C_G(z)=(T\rtimes\langle c\rangle)/Z$ is
the monomial group of order $3(q\mp1)^2/3$, together with one element
$g_y\in N_G(U)$ with $z^{g_y}=y$ for each $y$ in the $N_G(U)$-orbit of $z$;
$g_y=hg_0$ with $g_0$ an explicit conjugator $z\to y$ (a matrix of
eigenvectors of a lift of $y$, normalised to determinant $1$ and, in the
unitary case, to orthonormal rows) and $h\in C_G(z)$ a conjugator of $U$ to
$U^{g_0^{-1}}\le C_G(z)$.  The extensions by the outer automorphism are
handled in $6$-dimensional matrix models.  The systems so obtained were
checked to be saturated with $O_3(\F)=1=O^3(\F)$ by the package before the
isomorphism test.  (This computation uncovered a bug in the isomorphism
test of the package: two systems whose stored lists of essential subgroups
contained different numbers of $S$-class representatives of the same
$\F$-class were declared non-isomorphic.  It is fixed, the test suite
passes, and none of the results reported elsewhere in this document used
the affected code path.)

\emph{Exotic systems.}  To our knowledge the almost simple groups with a
Sylow $3$-subgroup isomorphic to $S$ are $\PSL_3(q)$, $\PSU_3(q)$ (with
$3^4\,\|\,q\mp1$) and their extensions by an outer automorphism of order
$2$, so that $\F_2$, $\F_3$, $\F_4$, $\F_6$ and $\F_7$ are candidates for
exotic fusion systems.  Saturated fusion systems on $3$-groups of maximal
class were classified by D\'{\i}az, Ruiz and Viruel (\emph{All $p$-local
finite groups of rank two for odd prime $p$}, Trans.\ AMS 359 (2007)), who
found exotic systems on these groups; \textcolor{red}{the comparison of the
five systems with their table is still to be made (the paper could not be
accessed from the computing environment).}

\subsection{\texttt{SmallGroup(6561,3016)}: two maximal subgroups as candidates, no fusion system}
\label{sec:3016}

$S=\texttt{SmallGroup(6561,3016)}$ has $|\Z(S)|=27$, class $3$, three
generators, exponent $9$ and $|\Aut(S)|=2^3\cdot3^{15}$.  Its only
proto-essential candidates are two maximal subgroups $E_1$, $E_2$ (of
ranks $4$ and $5$) with $\Out_S(E_i)\cong C_3$, and
$|\Out(E_1)|=2^6\cdot3^{15}\cdot13$ (with a section $\PSL_3(3)$),
$|\Out(E_2)|=2^6\cdot3^{12}$ (soluble).  Neither has an automizer
candidate: there is no subgroup of $\Out(E_i)$ with $\Out_S(E_i)$ as a
Sylow $3$-subgroup and a strongly $3$-embedded subgroup (the
$3$-soluble candidates are enumerated completely by the method of
Section~\ref{sec:lookup}, the others by the perfect subgroup method).
Hence there is no fusion system ($38$ seconds; the first attempt with the
subgroup lattice of $\Out(E_2)$ ran for more than $40$ minutes).

\subsection{Groups whose proto-essential subgroups are $3^4$ with $|N_S(E):E|=3$}
\label{sec:tier81}

Among the first $92$ groups passing Stage 1b, six have proto-essential
